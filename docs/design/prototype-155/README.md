# Prototype #155 — the mark, and what it weighs

Throwaway prototype for
[Design Pass: The Mark and the Icon Rule](https://github.com/amin-bf/cairn/issues/155), split out of
[The Craft](https://github.com/amin-bf/cairn/issues/149). Preserved as the tag
`prototypes/issue-155`, contained in no branch. Reachable from any clone:

```sh
git show prototypes/issue-155:docs/design/prototype-155/README.md
git checkout prototypes/issue-155 -- crates/app/src/proto.rs
```

The decision it produced is [ADR-0038](../../adr/0038-the-mark-and-the-icon-rule.md).

## What it was not for

The **route** — icons are glyphs in the font stack — and the **placement** — the mark goes large
above *All caught up.* — were settled by [The Craft](https://github.com/amin-bf/cairn/issues/149) and
were not re-opened here. Neither was the **face**: it was built before the sitting started, and
building it *is* the ticket's first question answered.

`crates/app/assets/CairnIcons-Regular.ttf` is 952 bytes, generated by `scripts/build-icon-face.py`
from the launcher's own `crates/app/res/drawable/ic_launcher_monochrome.xml`, registered by
`fonts::install` into all three families, and drawn through `ui.visuals().text_color()`. No image, no
crate, no layout code, and `--check` rebuilds it and diffs — so *the glyph is the launcher's four
stones* is a claim a command answers.

## The finding that came before any candidate

**A glyph's line box is the family's, not the glyph's**, and it cost 63px of space nobody chose.

`ui.label` allocates `Fonts::row_height`, which is the **tallest face in the family** at that size.
The icon face declares a cap-height ascent and a shallow descent and gets no say in it. Measured
through `run_ui`:

| asked for | stones drawn | row allocated | air above | air below |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 30px | 46px | 3px | 13px |
| 100 | 73px | 115px | 8px | 34px |
| 150 | 109px | 172px | 10px | **53px** |

The skirt scales with the size, so a knob setting the mark would have been dragging **two** distances
at once with only one of them visible, and the gap the ADR named would not have been the gap on
screen. That is [ADR-0032 §2](../../adr/0032-the-type-scale-and-the-rhythm.md)'s *a stated gap is the
whole gap* broken by a quantity nobody wrote down.

`crate::icon` allocates the **ink** and paints the galley offset into it. **Inline the line box is
exactly right** — an icon beside the word it illustrates wants to sit on that word's line, which is
the whole argument for glyphs — so this is the one case the route does not cover rather than a
retreat from it. ADR-0038 §4.

## The knobs, and why none of them was a menu

[#141](https://github.com/amin-bf/cairn/issues/141)'s finding, applied a third time: *judging a
distance wants a knob, not a menu*. All three quantities here are distances.

| knob | range | opened at | **landed on** |
|---|---|---|---|
| size | 40–300 | 150 | **104** — 75px of stones |
| gap to the sentence | `gap(0)`–`gap(8)`, snapped | `gap(3)` | **`gap(8)`** — 64px |
| ink | alpha 0–255 on `text_color()` | 255 | **147**, recorded as **153** |

**The ink knob was an alpha rather than a choice between two roles, and that is what produced the
answer.** The palette's two existing roles sit on the same scale — `text_color()` at 255,
`weak_text_color()` at 153 — both marked on the readout. The thumb stopped at **147**, six steps
from the weak role. Measured off the two captures: the stones are `#8d9293` at 147 and `#929697` at
153, a peak difference of **6 of 255 across the whole frame**, on a large flat grey.

So the answer is *the existing role serves*, and it is a finding rather than an assumption. A menu of
two would have offered `weak_text_color()` and got a yes, and nobody would have known whether the yes
meant *this weight* or *the nearer of your two*. The knob answered a question the menu could not
ask — and it is the reverse of the shape #141 met, where a knob was needed because no candidate was
right.

### The ink

[255 at size 150](ink-1280x800/20-ink-255.png) is the naive reading of the ticket — *large*, and *it

### Both themes, one construction

The close condition was that the mark needs **one** construction rather than a per-theme pair, and
confirming it rather than assuming it was the point — #143's finding is that a rule can pass while
its values fail. It holds: the same expression at the same alpha, in
[light at 1280](themes/40-light-1280x800.png) and [light at 560](themes/41-light-560x860.png).

## The two toggles

### Where the block sits — **today**, unchanged

`gap(8)` under the heading was kept over [optical centring in the page
room](size-1280x800/13-block-centred.png). The question is one the mark *created* rather than
inherited: a single sentence tucked under a heading is defensible, and a three-times-taller block in
the same place is a different claim. It was answered by looking, and the answer was that the block
still belongs at the top.

`gap(8)` therefore appears **twice** on this screen — the lead under the heading and the mark to the
sentence — which reads as rhythm rather than collision and is recorded so the next person does not
take it for a copy-paste.

### The leech entrance — **ADR-0035 §1 becomes a page rule**

[Today](entrance-1280x800/30-under-the-statement.png) the entrance sits `gap(3)` under the statement,
bottom edge at y=305 of 800. [§1 applied](entrance-1280x800/31-on-the-reach-line.png) puts its bottom
edge on the reach line, 165px above the page bottom.

This screen is the **first with cause to apply §1 or ignore it anywhere but Review** —
`frame::slack_above` had exactly one call site, and the caught-up screen with a leech is the only
state in the application where this screen carries a control at all. §1 wins: it is a page rule, and
`slack_above` gains its second call site. What was forbidden was leaving §1 standing as written while
the app did otherwise.

## Running it

The knobs are on Settings, directly under the heading and **above Appearance** — that position is
forced rather than preferred, for the reason
[#154](https://github.com/amin-bf/cairn/issues/154) measured: Appearance's sentence wraps at 560 and
not at 1280, so anything a storyboard must click at both judging widths has to sit above it.

**`cairn-fixture` wipes the platform data directory before installing**, so redirect the XDG bases or
it takes the operator's own collection with it:

```sh
export XDG_DATA_HOME=/tmp/cairn-proto155/data XDG_STATE_HOME=/tmp/cairn-proto155/state
mkdir -p "$XDG_DATA_HOME" "$XDG_STATE_HOME"
cargo build -p cairn-desktop
./target/debug/cairn-fixture caught-up    # or `leeches`, for the entrance question
./target/debug/cairn
```

`CAIRN_PROTO=size,gap,ink,centred,reach` sets the knobs from the environment, which is how the
captures above were taken — a still of a knob position nobody can reproduce is worth nothing.
`scripts/storyboards/proto-155-light.txt` is the light run, and it needs its own y coordinate rather
than `light.txt`'s: the switcher pushes Appearance down the page, and clicking 93 would have produced
a full set of valid **dark** captures under light names.
