Take the Settings Slice — sitting 1

These are wireframes drawn at the app's real sizes and colours: the 640 column, the 28 margin, the 8 grid, 15/12/20 type, 36 controls, the dark palette and the shipped fonts. They are not builds. Settings is drawn whole, as one tall picture: the orange dashed line is where the window ends, and everything under it is reached by scrolling. Each selector above a drawing changes it in place, and the questions at the bottom are the same choices, with my recommendation marked.

Words used on this page

block
one group on Settings: a small grey label, its controls, a line under it
specimen
a development block that only existed to test something: the rendering specimen (fonts, #97) and the hand-off specimen (writing a file and sharing it, #98). Both tickets are closed
fixture
a pre-made collection that replaces yours so a screen can be photographed (nothing due, leeches, a backlog…)
file set
a pre-made batch of files written into the file list, for the same reason
fixture bench
the block of fixture and file-set buttons. On the phone it's the only way to install one, and a script drives it
storyboard
a script of clicks the capture harness plays to take screenshots. It clicks at fixed positions, so moving a block means re-measuring them
sub-state
a second screen drawn inside Settings without a nav entry, like enrolment (setting up sync)
reach line
the line 165px above the bottom of the window where a screen's last control sits, measured by thumb (ADR-0035)
primary
the one filled control on a screen with no card: the way forward (ADR-0034 §2)
text action
an unframed alternative beside a primary, like Cancel (ADR-0034 §5)

  1. Q1. Both specimens answered their tickets. The Kotlin client will need its own test bench anyway, so these don't carry over to it. Removing the hand-off specimen leaves the share call (hand_off) with no caller until the export screen exists. That screen is out of scope here.
  2. Q2. Reset and the bench still have work to do: the phone has no other way to install a fixture, and fixture-bench.txt drives them. With A, the shipped Settings never shows them, and a build made with a bench switch adds them as one section at the end. Use the second selector to flip between the two builds.
  3. Q3. The code says everything below Appearance syncs, and that's false. Only New cards a day and the Scheduler follow your collection to another device. Appearance, the sync connection and the files belong to this machine. B makes that visible with two bold group headings. Any order other than today's means re-measuring the storyboard clicks at a fixed 1280×800, the way file-surface.txt already does. It's mechanical work, and I'd do it.
  4. Q6. ADR-0035 §1 (as amended by ADR-0040 §4) says the last control on a screen sits on the reach line, and ADR-0039 §8 says a scrolling screen meets it only by pinning that control outside the scroll. Read literally, Settings must pin whatever happens to come last. Flip to B to see what that looks like: a list row or the appearance switch, cut off from its block and kept on screen for no reason. A amends §1 to bind a screen's closing control (the one pressed to finish or go on: the grades, Done, the import gate, the leech entrance). A page of peer settings has no closing control, but its sub-states do (enrolment, a sync screen), and those take the line.
