Take the Review Slice

Four structurally different takes on the Review screen, built as a throwaway egui binary and photographed through the headless harness at both judging widths. Nothing here is committed to the app. The point is to have something concrete to react to — and then to decide how this ticket gets split, because it is too big as one.

What the baseline actually shows

Before any of the variants: the single largest defect in the Review screen today is not on this ticket's list. The ticket names palette, type scale, spacing, radius, weight, motion and elevation as the foundations, and the picker, dashboard, grade buttons and empty state as the arrangement. What the captures show is that the screen has no page frame at all — no margin, no measure. Content runs edge to edge at 1280 exactly as it does at 560.

At 1280 that means a card 1280px wide with one word centred in it, and four grade bars each a metre long. The map's fog already suspected this — “one responsive design currently means one design, stretched” — but it reads as a *responsive* problem there, and it is not. It is a missing foundation, and it is missing at every width.

The leeches question found a real bug, not a preference.

The durable leech entrance is offered whenever any card is a leech or is suspended, and the leech screen is those cards' permanent home (ADR-0010 §6, §8). On a caught-up Review it is the only control on the screen — so B's empty state, which dropped it, was not a quieter design but an unreachable one. Only variant C happened to keep it. E restores it, below the sentence and quiet, per §6's “never competes with the picker”. Worth carrying forward: the empty state is the one screen where the leech entrance has no competition, and three of five variants lost it without anything failing.

The four it was grafted from

Each variant carries a complete set of foundation tokens rather than sharing one, because a hero-card layout and a dense two-column layout do not want the same type scale — and pretending they do is how a scale ends up decided in the abstract, which is the cost ADR-0030 records. Colour is held constant across all of them so that what you are looking at is arrangement, type and rhythm.

What the images establish

  1. The frame is the lever, and it is not on the ticket's list. A page margin and a capped measure account for most of the visible distance between the baseline and every variant. It belongs in the foundations half and is missing from it.
  2. The palette is not the problem. All four variants use ADR-0030's colours unchanged and all four read markedly better than the baseline. The ticket expects to supersede ADR-0030; on this evidence the Review slice gives no reason to.
  3. The type scale is the second lever. Stock egui's 12.5px body and 9px small were never decided — the app simply inherited them. Lifting the card face to a display tier is what makes the card read as the thing being studied rather than as a label.
  4. The card currently reads as a button. It is drawn on the inactive widget fill, which is lighter than the panel, so it sits on the page like a control. B's well — darker than the page — reads as a surface to read into.
  5. Prompt and answer are one thing. A's two equal slabs read as two objects; B, C and D's single card divided by a hairline reads as two faces of one card, which is what it is.
  6. “Review” as a heading duplicates the “Review” nav tab. Visible in A, C and D. One of them should go.
  7. Two grades sharing 1d looks like a fault when label and interval are set at the same size and weight. Demoting the interval to the small tier fixes the reading without touching the fact.
  8. 1280 genuinely has nothing to do unless an arrangement changes. A, B and D centre a column and leave roughly half the window empty. Only C spends it. This is the real content of “one responsive design” for this screen, and it is a decision, not a defect.

Two harness bugs were found and fixed on the way. The split

The ticket says: “This may well split once its size is real. If it does, split it — do not quietly do half.” Its size is now real. Held against the convergence rule — a ticket closes only when the ADR, the design project and the running app agree — the honest count is four tickets, not one, and they stay in the Review slice rather than becoming a foundations-first detour.
